# Hackers 2026 rc5: flight schools from the gap-source leads, each confirmed on its own pages

Generated by `research/FlightSchoolMapping/scripts/67_lead_schools.py report` on 2026-11-06; do not edit by hand. Stage: prototype work inside an MVP release (`docs/hackers-2026-plan.md`). One disposition per lead is in [`hackers-2026-rc5-additions.csv`](hackers-2026-rc5-additions.csv), the hand check in [`hackers-2026-rc5-handcheck.csv`](hackers-2026-rc5-handcheck.csv).

> **Headline.** 2,056 rows from three lead lists came down to 1,402 distinct leads after rule drops and de-duplication, and to **156 flight-training operations added**, each confirmed on its own website (131) or on another public page that names it (25). `flight_line.json` would go from 5,458 to 5,614 records. By lead source (a school can have several): Tach & Go 102, PPP 10, master list public rows 107, master list AOPA-only rows 39 (39 reached only through an AOPA-only lead). The staged release has **2,911 school pages** (rc4: 2,836): 88 added schools list a fleet or prices, and 13 schools whose domain now serves gambling, parking or other unrelated content were taken out. Hand check: **29 of 30** additions correct, after a first round at 25 of 30 tightened the rule for schools with no website. Cost **$13.53** of the $15 cap. Nothing is published.

## Why

The gap-source passes (`hackers-2026-gap-sources.md`, `hackers-2026-master-list-check.md`) estimated that a few hundred real training operations on outside lists are missing from ours. This pass checks every one of those leads against the operator's own pages and adds the ones that hold up, for release rc5. Another approach would serve if it adds only operations confirmed from their own public pages, keeps everything AOPA-derived out of stored and published values, and leaves existing records unchanged.

## Method

1. **Search** (`67 search`), website-less leads only: Claude Haiku 5.5 with Anthropic's web search tool, one search per lead, `aopa.org` blocked, effort low, asked for the operator's own website or another public page that shows it (an airport page, an EAA chapter, an association list, a news story), with Facebook and directory pages reported but not used. A site the model named is crawled; its own pages decide. Lead names go into the search and into dedupe only.
2. **Crawl** the websites (from the lists and from search): `09a_fleet_crawl.py --targets --out-dir` with 09a's identified user agent, then `10_fleet_browser_crawl.py --crawl-only --no-evasion --user-agent "AetherResearch/1.0 (...)"` for thin, refused or missing HTTP crawls (60's rule). The browser crawl sent the same identified user agent, not a desktop Chrome one (`--user-agent` is new in 10 for this). Nothing tried to get past a bot check; refusals and challenges are counted. Public pages found by search were fetched once each with 65's identified user agent.
3. **Classify** (`67 classify-build`, Message Batches, Haiku 5.5, effort medium): 60's prompt and schema on each site's own pages, named by domain only; a variant for the public pages that asks about the named business only. Every value returned (name, phone, street address, hours, city and state, the evidence quote) must be found verbatim on a page the request carried (`page_text.py`), or it is dropped; a public-page answer of "yes" without a verbatim quote becomes "unclear".
4. **Dedupe** (`67 dedupe`): 60's strict rules against `flight_line.json` (same domain or redirect, near-identical name in the state, same street address with a shared name word), then between leads. Domains already ours were dropped before crawling.
5. **Hand check**: a seeded random 30 of the additions read against their pages (`67 sample --n 30 --seed 20261106`).
6. **Add** (`67 ingest`): `output/added_schools_rc5.json`, a separate file that `21_build_flight_line.py` merges like 57's and 60's entries, with source `rc5_leads`, per-field provenance (`site` or `public_page`, the page URL and its crawl date) and the lead ids in `provenance` (public-list ids, and the master list's own entity ids; no AOPA values). Independent instructors get no street address (38's policy, now also reading this pass's CSV); public-page schools get no phone or street address.
7. **Extract** fleets and prices with `extract_v2.py` (the March prompt), Haiku 5.5, merged by `57 merge-extract`; `61_contact_provenance.py report` takes the verified contact values; 21 and 22 rebuild `flight_line.json`.

## Airports for the additions

`67 airports` gives each addition the airport its cited pages name, recorded in the school's `locations` with provenance (the page, the quoted text, how it was matched, and the row it matched). **131 of 156** are matched (51 had an `airport_code` in the staged file before, all still the same airport); 25 are not. `21_build_flight_line.py` puts the match on the record: on the fleet extraction's location when that names the same airport, else first. In the rebuilt `flight_line.json`, 137 of the 156 records have a valid airport (97 in the first rc5 build), 131 with this provenance and 6 only from the extraction; 19 have none. Existing schools' airports are untouched (`57 check`).

Order of matching, each on text found again verbatim on a page the classifier read (no page was refetched): (1) **code**, an identifier the pages give, alone or in parentheses after a name, found in `output/airports.csv` and in the school's state; (2) **name**, an airport name the pages give whose words (less generic ones such as Airport, Field, Regional) equal exactly one airport's in the state, or a full airport name from the table for that state found on the pages when only one is; with no state, only an exact full name unique in the whole table; (3) **city**, the city and state the pages give with exactly one open public-use airport in FAA NASR (2026-10-29); (4) **model**, Haiku 5.5 choosing from the state's open airports, seaplane bases and gliderports, kept only if its quote is on the page and names that candidate and no other. The state is the page's, else a public (non-AOPA) lead list's, used for matching only. Stored identifiers use `airports.csv`'s `airport_id` (Meadow Lake, FAA FLY, is `K00V`), as the rest of `flight_line.json` does.

## Things that look wrong (found on the way)

- **Correction: "105 of 156 additions have no airport" was wrong.** It counted the staged file's `airport_code` only; 21 also takes airports from the fleet extraction, so the first rc5 build already had a valid airport on 97 of the 156 records (none with provenance, some with malformed identifiers such as `K3AU`). After `67 airports`: 137 of 156 have a valid airport, 131 of them with page-and-quote provenance (6 only from the extraction); 19 have none. On the release's pages, 81 of the 88 added schools list an airport (71 before).
- **Public-page schools carry almost nothing.** The 25 schools added from an airport or state page have a name and provenance but no phone or street address (by rule) and usually no city: the page rarely says, and the lead lists' states were not used for stored values.
- **Tails shared with records we already had** (26): most are FAA registrant records that look like leasebacks or owners (an LLC or a person); five are one tail shared with another school's website record (phx-afc.com and maf-club.com, bakersfieldflyingclub.com and chickadeeaviation.com, flyathena.com and savannahaviation.com, wetsuaviation.com and backcountryaviation.net, stuartflyingclub.org and rightrudderaviation.com). Left for review, not merged.
- **The web search was most of the spend.** $12.40 of the $13.51 went to 1,067 synchronous searches at standard price (web search has no batch discount); 260 of the searched leads had no public source and 151 only directory listings.
- **Clubs are judged two ways.** The site prompt (60's) answers "no" for a flying club whose pages describe aircraft access but no instruction, so such clubs were rejected (counted above); the public-page prompt accepts a club whose members fly club aircraft. Some public-page clubs may therefore be included that the site rule would have left out. One rule should be chosen for both.
- **140 school domains have lapsed**, far more than the two known cases; five live school sites carry injected gambling links and stay.
- **`build_rc5.sh` named a build directory that no longer existed** (`aether-desc-build`); it was recreated as a detached worktree of aether-addschools before the build.
