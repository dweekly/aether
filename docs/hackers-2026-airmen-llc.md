# Hackers 2026: the pilot at the LLC's address, from the FAA airmen file

Generated by `research/FlightSchoolMapping/scripts/58_airmen_llc_join.py --registry 2026-10-23`. Regenerate rather than edit.
FAA releasable airmen file `CS112026.zip` (https://registry.faa.gov/database/CS112026.zip, file created November 1, 2026, Last-Modified Sun, 01 Nov 2026 14:56:30 GMT, sha256 `7c28353c92a8c081...`): 593,424 airmen in the pilot file, 576,567 with a US street address, 401,096 of those holding a pilot certificate (any grade, students included) and 64,366 a current flight-instructor certificate. FAA registry snapshot 2026-10-23, valid registrations with a US street address and ZIP.

**Headline:** 17,362 of 86,805 LLC-registered aircraft (20.0%) have a certificated pilot at exactly the registry's mailing address, as do 6,545 of 60,595 corporation-registered aircraft (10.8%). With every airman's house number shuffled within the ZIP code, the same join finds 21 LLC aircraft (0.02%). Where a pilot is found, the pilot's surname is in the LLC's name 12.5% of the time against 0.0% for another LLC's name, and in Florida, where the state's corporate file names the LLC's managers, a matched pilot's surname is among them 86.0% of the time against 0.1% for another LLC's managers.

**Against Florida:** Florida's corporate file names a person for 82.5% of Florida-address LLC aircraft (52); the airmen file finds a pilot at the address for 22.4% of the same aircraft, against 20.0% for LLCs nationally. Together they reach 85.7%: the airmen route adds 236 aircraft the corporate file doesn't name. It needs no state data, so it works in the states whose corporate files are not free or do not list managers, but in Florida it covers 27% as many aircraft as the corporate file.

**What a match is, and isn't:** a pilot who gets mail where the LLC does is a probable principal, family member or employee, not proof of ownership or of who flies the aircraft. It is a lower bound: airmen who asked the FAA to withhold their address are absent from the file entirely, and so are owners who are not pilots or whose LLC uses an agent's or office address.

## Validation: is the matched pilot plausibly behind the registrant?

For each matched aircraft, does a matched pilot's surname (3+ letters, JR/SR/II/III/IV removed) appear in the registrant name or the registry's other-owner names? Control: the same pilots' surnames checked against another matched aircraft's names, as in 52. All matched aircraft are checked, not a sample. LLC and corporation names usually carry no surname, so their agreement is low in absolute terms; the test is agreement against control. Individuals are the positive reference: their registrant name is a person.

## Method

- **Address key.** Street 1, upper-cased; periods and apostrophes dropped, other punctuation turned into spaces, letters and digits split apart (12A, 12-A and 12 A agree), spaces collapsed; common street suffixes, directionals and unit words abbreviated the USPS way (STREET to ST, NORTH to N, SUITE to STE); P.O. box spellings unified to PO BOX; plus the 5-digit ZIP. Street 2 is ignored; unit numbers written in street 1 must agree. US addresses only on both sides.
- **What the standardization does.** With 52's key (punctuation dropped, nothing abbreviated) 27,321 LLC/corporation aircraft match a pilot; the standardized key matches 27,539 before exclusions (18,777 LLC aircraft, 21.6%).
- **Shared addresses.** 147,400 LLC/corporation aircraft sit at 66,715 distinct addresses. 14,901 aircraft (10.1%) use a P.O. box and 4,400 an address with no number at all. 22,771 aircraft (15.4%) are at an address used by more than 5 distinct registrant names in the whole registry: registered agents, law firms, management companies. On the airmen side, 193 addresses hold more than 5 airmen (3,264 airmen in all): airports, bases, schools, crew mail drops. Matches at either kind of address are excluded; that drops 3,632 LLC/corporation aircraft from the matched count. P.O. boxes are kept (2,213 matched aircraft) because a box number plus ZIP is as specific as a street address.
- **Why those cut-offs.** Both were set at 5 before the run. Surname agreement for matched LLC/corporation aircraft, before exclusion, by how many distinct registrant names share the address and how many airmen do. Agreement holds flat up to five registrant names and collapses past twenty (registered agents and mail-forwarding addresses that some pilots also use as their own); on the airmen side it falls from the first bucket on, so a tighter airmen cut-off would also be defensible:

| Registrant names at the address | Matched aircraft | Surname agreement |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 15,031 | 13.4% |
| 2-5 | 9,985 | 13.5% |
| 6-20 | 1,131 | 8.1% |
| 21+ | 1,392 | 0.0% |

| Airmen at the address | Matched aircraft | Surname agreement |
|---|---|---|
| 1-2 | 25,454 | 13.1% |
| 3-5 | 570 | 8.2% |
| 6-20 | 820 | 3.3% |
| 21+ | 695 | 6.2% |

- **Who counts.** A pilot is any airman with a type P certificate (student, sport, recreational, private, commercial, ATP); airmen with only a remote-pilot (drone) certificate are not counted. An instructor holds a flight-instructor certificate whose expiry date is on or after the file date.
- **Negative control.** Each airman's house number (the first number in the address key, or the box number of a P.O. box) is given to the next airman in a seeded random order within the same ZIP code, so the street names, ZIP codes and numbers present are unchanged and only which number goes with which street is broken. Airmen whose number would not change are left out rather than kept at their real address, and so is any shuffled address that turns out to be another airman's real address (13,019 of 564,416), because a match there is a real pilot's match carried into the control: P.O. boxes at one post office are numbered densely, and fly-in communities put many pilots on one street. 551,397 of 576,567 airmen remain. The same join and exclusions are then run.
- **The control as first run.** Keeping those addresses, the shuffled join matched 1,040 LLC aircraft and 656 corporation aircraft (1.20% and 1.08%), and its planned gate (control under a tenth of real) failed for corporations at 10.0x. Dropping the 2.3% of shuffled addresses that are some other airman's real address removed 98.0% of its LLC hits. Those hits are real pilot addresses that the real join already counts, reached with the wrong pilot: the shuffled pilot's surname is in the LLC's name for 0.8% of them, against 12.5% for real matches (validation table).
- **What the control does and doesn't show.** It shows that exact matches are not string coincidences between unrelated addresses in the same ZIP code. It is generous to the real join in one way: many shuffled addresses don't exist (a house number past the end of the street), and a non-existent address can't match anything. So the evidence that a matched pilot is tied to the registrant rests on the surname checks above, Florida's officer check most of all, not on the address control alone.
- **Prior probe.** `beneficial_airmen_join.py` (2026-10-27, October file, 52's key, no exclusions, remote-pilot-only airmen counted as pilots, control = the same street in the neighbouring ZIP) found 23.7% of non-Montana LLC aircraft at a pilot's address (`docs/data-merge-research-plan.md` on `hackers-airports`). Here the same cohort is 20.2%; the drop is the exclusions and the narrower definition of a pilot.

## Caveats

- **Opt-in bias.** The releasable file holds only airmen who did not ask the FAA to withhold their address. It lists 416,672 pilot-certificate holders and 69,533 flight instructors; the FAA's [2025 airmen statistics](https://www.faa.gov/data_research/aviation_data_statistics/civil_airmen_statistics/2025_Active_Civil_Airmen_Statistics_FINAL.xlsx) count 887,519 active pilots and 145,538 flight-instructor certificates at the end of 2025, so the file holds roughly 47% and 48% of them. The two counts are ten months apart and the FAA's "active" requires a current medical, so the ratio is only a rough size of the opt-out. Privacy-minded owners, the ones most likely to register through an LLC, are also the most likely to withhold, so the match rate understates who is behind LLCs and is not a random sample of them.
- **Mailing address, not home.** The registry address is where the FAA sends mail. A match at an office (a corporation's headquarters where a company pilot gets mail) is a real link but not an owner. Excluding heavily shared addresses removes most offices, not all.
- **Exact match only.** Different spellings of the same address that the standardization doesn't fix (FIRST vs 1ST, a unit in street 2 on one side) are missed, so this undercounts.
- **One snapshot each.** The airmen file is from November 1, 2026; the registry from 2026-10-23. People move.
- **Privacy.** This doc and the committed script carry aggregate counts only. The tail-to-airman join (`join_raw.parquet`) stays in the git-ignored data directory.
